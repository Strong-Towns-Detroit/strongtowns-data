# Acquisition pathway matrix

`ACQ-002` · acquisition · service code `LND`

> **Reusable.** One copy project-wide, under `working/project-wide/`.

## Purpose

Compares every route by which Krabby could acquire a buildable Detroit parcel, so that the
pathway is chosen deliberately rather than by whichever listing surfaced first. Each pathway
has different eligibility, pricing, approval authority, and — most consequentially —
different post-closing obligations.

> **Currency.** External requirements verified **2026-08-26**. The governing DLBA policy was
> adopted **2025-11-11** and is recent; re-verify before relying on any row.

> **This is not legal advice.** It is an issue-spotting tool. Conveyance structure, deed
> restrictions, and reverter rights require review by a Michigan-licensed attorney.

## The headline finding

**The pathway that matters for new home construction is the Infill Housing Lot programme**
(`SRC-001` Ch. VI), not Side Lots, Neighborhood Lots, or "Create-a-Project". Two corrections
to the common understanding of this landscape:

1. **Side Lots and Neighborhood Lots cannot be used to build.** Both are adjacency-gated
   programmes for existing Detroit homeowners buying the lot next door. Side Lots require the
   purchaser to already hold title to a Street Adjacent occupied residential property
   (Ch. I(C)(1)); Neighborhood Lots require a Principal Residence Exemption. Neither is
   available to a developer acquiring land to construct on. These were excluded from scope at
   the outset and the research confirms the exclusion was correct.

2. **"Create-a-Project" does not mean what the website implies.** The adopted *Neighborhood
   Create-a-Project Pilot Policy* (`SRC-002`) makes vacant land available to **neighborhood
   community groups and block clubs** — an existing legal entity such as a partnership, LLC or
   corporation — for **public benefit projects: art installations, playgrounds, community
   gardens** (Sec. I, Sec. II(A)). Lots are $250, capped at four at a time (Sec. IV(A)-(B)),
   must already be designated Neighborhood Lots (Sec. III(A)), and carry a three-year maintain
   obligation with a reverter (Sec. V(A)). **There is no housing construction use.** The site's
   `/Create-a-Project` navigation link nevertheless resolves to the general development
   application page (`SRC-004`). Treat the label as ambiguous and confirm which programme is
   meant before applying.

### 1. DLBA Infill Housing Lot — the primary route

*Objective, per Ch. VI(A): "To encourage construction of new residential housing, including
affordable housing, in the City."*

| Dimension | Requirement | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lot must be | Vacant residential, no structure; may or may not be adjacent to an occupied structure | VI(B)(1)–(2) |
| Taxes | No delinquent or currently due property taxes on the lot | VI(B)(3) |
| Zoning | R1, R1-H, R2, R3, R3H, R4, R5, R5-H, R6, R6-H, PD, PD-H, SD1, SD1-H, SD2, SD2-H, SD4 | VI(B)(4) |
| Size | **Must not exceed 7,500 sq ft** | VI(B)(5) |
| Location | **Must be within an Inclusive Housing Opportunity Area**, or another area approved by a City Revitalization Office | VI(B)(6) |
| Pre-listing | City planning or economic development agencies must approve the listing before sale | VI(B)(7) |
| Purchaser must | Use the lot to develop new housing; provide a thorough description of the proposed development | VI(C)(1)–(2) |
| Capacity | Demonstrate sufficient capacity to **finance and complete** a 1–4 unit residential project within a reasonable time | VI(C)(3) |
| Tax standing | Current on taxes for **all** Detroit property owned directly or indirectly | VI(C)(4) |
| Volume cap | **Not more than three Infill Housing Lots in the preceding twelve months** | VI(C)(5) |
| DLBA standing | Good standing on any DLBA agreement | VI(C)(6) |
| Pricing | Fair market value as determined by an independent real estate broker | VI(D) |
| Discount | 50% off if ≥25% of units affordable at ≤80% AMI; 80% off if ≥25% at ≤50% AMI — **rental units only**, with a **ten-year** affordability agreement and City monitoring | VI(D)(1) |
| Discount cap | Cumulative discount may not exceed 90% | VI(D)(2) |
| Listing period | Listed at least 15 days before an offer is selected | VI(E)(1) |
| Selection | Comprehensive process weighing offer price, community benefit, purchaser experience and financial capability, connection to the neighborhood, proposed use, and support of neighbors and local organizations | VI(E)(2) |
| Side Lot priority | If also Side Lot eligible, offered as a Side Lot for 30 days first | VI(E)(3) |
| **Compliance** | Purchaser **must enter a development agreement** with DLBA setting type, density, required schedules and timelines, and affordability monitoring. **DLBA reserves the right to take back title** on violation | VI(F) |
| Tax capture | Five-year, fifty-percent tax capture. Waiver is **not free** on this pathway — see below | VI(G), `SRC-013` IV(C) |

**Three constraints that shape the whole programme, not just one parcel:**

- The **7,500 sq ft ceiling** (VI(B)(5)) caps single-lot yield. A larger site requires
  assemblage, which means multiple transactions and multiple development agreements.
- The **three-lot / twelve-month cap** (VI(C)(5)) is a *pipeline* constraint. It must be
  tracked centrally in `SCR-005`, not per parcel. The policy says "directly or indirectly"
  without defining affiliation — see open questions.
- **Inclusive Housing Opportunity Areas** are defined as areas where on-market home sales have
  averaged **$100/sq ft or more over the last 12 months** (Ch. IX(B)(1)). Infill Housing Lots
  exist only there or in City-Revitalization-approved areas. This is a hard geographic filter
  on `SCR-004`, and it is recomputed at least annually (Ch. IX(C)).

**Tax capture is a real cost on this pathway.** The DLBA holds a statutory right to 50% of
property taxes for five years (MCL 211.7gg), waivable under MCL 211.1025a(1). `SRC-013`
Sec. IV(A)–(B) waives it *free of charge* for Side, Accessory Structure, Oversize and
Neighborhood Lots, and for Auction and Own-It-Now purchases. Sec. IV(C) covers "Other Programs
(including, but not limited to, Projects and **Infill Housing Lots**)" and waives the capture
**only if the owner compensates the DLBA in full** for the estimated remaining amount — or did
not receive reasonable advance notice that compensation might be required. Staff calculate the
amount **in their sole discretion** (Sec. VI(A)) and may demand pro formas, development plans
and timetables. A free waiver is available at staff discretion only where the estimated capture
is **under $250** (Sec. IV(D)). Denial is appealable to the Board once per property.

Model this as a buy-out line in `SCR-008` and `FIN-002`, not as contingency. Precisely the
programmes offering the deepest acquisition discounts are the ones where the tax capture must
be bought out.

### 2. DLBA Marketed Property

Listed 60 days through partner brokers; proposals forwarded to DLBA after the listing closes;
approximately 90 further days to closing. Scored on price, experience and financing, project
feasibility, and neighborhood benefit, with additional points for Office of Civil Rights
certification (`SRC-009`). A **"New Build Opportunities"** proposal-guideline category exists
but the guideline PDF was **not retrieved** — retrieve before preparing any proposal.

### 3–4. Purchase by Application (DLBA and City)

For property not listed on any platform. Submitted through the City's Public Property Purchase
Application portal (`SRC-010`). Expect a DLBA callback within 2–4 weeks if DLBA owns it
(`SRC-004`).

For **City-owned** property the rules differ materially (`SRC-008`): review involves the
Mayor's Office, HRD, P&DD, DEGC, Law, DBA, DLBA and Department of Neighborhoods, and **Detroit
City Council is the final decision maker**. Property is generally sold at market value; extremely
low offers are generally not accepted. Expect roughly **four months** application to close. A
development application requires at minimum a conceptual plan with proposed use, scope of work,
estimated costs and financing sources, and prior experience with similar developments.

### 5. Neighborhood Development Projects — the assemblage route

Adopted 11.11.2025, the same day as the Vacant Land Policy, and expressly **not** superseded by
it (`SRC-001` Intro p. iii). Sec. II qualifies parcels whose intended use includes **new
construction** and affordable housing, so this is a live pathway for Krabby — and the natural
one for anything multi-parcel or above the Infill Housing Lot's 7,500 sq ft ceiling.

| Dimension | Requirement | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified uses | Affordable housing, residential rehab, **new construction**, mixed-use, commercial, industrial, urban agriculture, beautification, green space | II |
| Zoning gate | Staff must determine the proposed use complies with zoning before sale. **Final closing can occur only if appropriate zoning is in place** | III(A)(1) |
| If zoning does not comply | DLBA **may grant an option agreement** over a specified period while the purchaser pursues rezoning, waiver/variance, or a zoning appeal | III(A)(2) |
| Option deposit | **Non-refundable**, the greater of $1,000 or 10% of the proposed purchase price | III(B)(1) |
| Agreement form | Purchase, development, or option agreement permitting DLBA to enforce development and use conditions; must have an explicit expiration date | III(B) |
| Pricing | Fair market value where practicable — internal comps, appraisals, BPOs, or a City EDO price | III(C), III(C)(1) |
| Price validity | Any price expires on a defined date **not exceeding 12 months**; once under agreement the price is fixed to closing | III(C)(2) |
| Pricing credits | Aggregate **maximum 40%**: adjacent-lot purchase 20%; special environmental considerations incl. demolition and remediation 20%; agricultural use 20% | III(C)(3)(b) |
| Credit exclusivity | Pricing discounts **cannot** be combined with these credits or other DLBA credits | III(C)(3)(c) |
| **Staff authority** | CEO or designee may transfer **up to 4 parcels** to the same transferee in any rolling 12 months, **or** any transaction with aggregate value **≤ $75,000** | V(A) |
| **Board approval required** | **5 or more parcels** to the same transferee in a rolling 12 months; **any transaction over $75,000**; any exception to the Policy | V(B) |

Two mechanisms here are strategically significant:

- **The option agreement (III(A)(2))** lets Krabby tie up a parcel whose current zoning does not
  permit the intended use while pursuing relief — at the cost of a non-refundable deposit of at
  least $1,000 or 10%. That converts a zoning problem from a disqualifier into a priced risk.
- **The $75,000 / 4-parcel threshold (V(A)–(B))** is the real schedule variable. Staying under
  it keeps a transaction at staff level; crossing it adds a Board cycle. Note this cap is
  *separate from and additional to* the Infill Housing Lot three-lot rule — an assemblage
  strategy has to be planned against both.

### 6–8. Private, direct, and assemblage

Carry no programme eligibility constraints, no development agreement, and no reverter — but
also no discount and no DLBA title clearing. `SRC-009` notes marketed DLBA properties have
clear title; a private parcel's title position must be established entirely through diligence.

Assemblage is a composite: each parcel follows its own pathway, and parcel combination is a
separate act (see `DD-009`). Note that **Privately Initiated Parcel Boundary Modifications made
after 1 January 2019 are disregarded in DLBA adjacency determinations** (`SRC-001` Ch. I(B)(4))
— combining lots privately cannot manufacture eligibility.
